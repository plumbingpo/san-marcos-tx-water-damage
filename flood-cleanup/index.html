A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. Day or night, 24/7, San Marcos Restoration Co sends flood cleanup crews throughout San Marcos to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. With the standing water gone, our IICRC-certified technicians check the contamination level and apply the right sanitizing steps, including antimicrobial treatment, HEPA air filtration and disinfection of every affected surface. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Stains along baseboards and lower walls that mark the flood level
  • Mud, silt or debris left on floors once the water goes down
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Soaked landscaping that leaves water pooling against your foundation
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • A battery-powered backup sump pump keeps water moving if the electricity fails in a storm
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Check that grading on all sides of the house carries water away from the foundation
