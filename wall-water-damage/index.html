Wall and Drywall Water Damage in San Marcos

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
