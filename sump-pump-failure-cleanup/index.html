Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in San Marcos: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.
  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Basement Floor Coverings

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.
