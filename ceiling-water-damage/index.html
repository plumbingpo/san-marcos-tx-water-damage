Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What San Marcos Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Stay out from under it and call.

What It Looks Like

  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • Stains or streaks running down the wall just below the ceiling line
  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
  • Bits of textured finish falling to the floor below a damp patch
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

Mold Above You

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. The paper layer becomes a place mold is able to grow, and heavy wet insulation bears down from above.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Saturated pieces may drop and carry soaked insulation down too. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
